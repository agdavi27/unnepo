Alumni are the most underrated resource in your job search. They chose the same school you did, they remember what being a student was like, and many of them genuinely want to help. Yet most students never reach out, because it feels awkward to message someone you've never met.

It doesn't have to be. Here's how to do it in a way that feels natural for both of you.

## Why alumni say yes

People like helping students from their own school. It's a small, meaningful favor, and it reminds them of their own early career. A message that starts "I'm a current student at your alma mater" gets opened far more often than a message from a stranger.

Alumni can also:

- Tell you what a job is *really* like.
- Explain how hiring works at their company.
- Point you to the right recruiter or open role.
- Sometimes refer you directly.

## How to find alumni

- **LinkedIn:** Go to your school's LinkedIn page and click **Alumni**. Filter by company, location, field of study or job function.
- **Your school's alumni directory** or mentoring platform, which many career centers offer.
- **Your career center**, which may know alumni who've volunteered to talk with students. See [how to get the most out of your career center](/guides/how-to-use-your-college-career-center).
- **Professors and coaches**, who often stay in touch with former students.

Prioritize recent graduates for internship advice. Someone two or three years out remembers the recruiting process clearly and is often more responsive than a senior executive.

## What to write

Keep your first message short, specific and easy to say yes to. Ask for advice, not a job.

### Template: LinkedIn message (under 300 characters)

> Hi Jamie, I'm a junior at Colby studying economics and I saw you're on the strategy team at Northwind. I'd love to hear how you got there. Would you be open to a 15-minute call sometime in the next few weeks?

### Template: Email

> **Subject:** Colby junior, quick question about your work at Northwind
>
> Hi Jamie,
>
> I'm a junior at Colby studying economics, and I found you through the alumni network. I'm interested in strategy roles and noticed you've been on Northwind's strategy team for two years.
>
> Would you be open to a 15-minute call in the next few weeks? I'd love to hear how you got started and what you wish you'd known as a student.
>
> I know you're busy, so I'm happy to work around your schedule.
>
> Thanks so much,
> Riley Chen

## What makes alumni messages work

- **Mention your shared school in the first line.** It's your strongest connection.
- **Be specific about why them.** Their role, team, company or career path.
- **Ask for a small amount of time.** Fifteen minutes is easy to agree to.
- **Don't ask for a job or referral in the first message.** That can come later, naturally.
- **Make scheduling easy.** Offer to work around them.

## If they don't reply

Follow up once, about a week later, in the same thread:

> Hi Jamie, just bumping this up in case it got buried. Totally understand if now isn't a good time!

If you still don't hear back, move on. People get busy; it's not personal.

## After the call

1. **Send a thank-you message the same day**, mentioning one specific thing you learned.
2. **Follow any advice they gave**, then let them know. ("I reached out to the recruiter you mentioned and have an interview next week. Thank you!")
3. **Stay in touch** with an occasional update every few months.

If the conversation goes well and there's a role you're applying to, it's natural to ask later: "Would you be comfortable referring me, or is there someone you'd suggest I talk to?" ## Frequently asked questions

### Is it okay to message alumni I don't know?

Yes. Alumni networks exist for exactly this reason, and many alumni enjoy helping students from their school. Keep your message short and respectful.

### What should I say when reaching out to alumni?

Mention your shared school, say why you're reaching out to them specifically, and ask for a short call. Ask for advice rather than a job.

### How do I find alumni at a specific company?

Use the Alumni tab on your school's LinkedIn page and filter by company, or check your school's alumni directory.

### Should I ask an alum for a referral?

Not in the first message. Build the relationship first; after a good conversation, asking whether they'd be comfortable referring you is reasonable.
