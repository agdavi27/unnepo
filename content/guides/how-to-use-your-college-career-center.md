Many students visit their career center once, maybe for a quick resume check, and never go back. That's a shame, because career centers offer far more than most students realize, and they're already included in your tuition.

Here's how to use yours well.

## What career centers actually offer

Services vary by school, but most offer:

- **Resume and cover letter reviews.**
- **Mock interviews**, including behavioral and case-style practice.
- **Career exploration** for when you're not sure what you want.
- **Alumni connections** and mentoring programs.
- **Job and internship databases**, often including Handshake.
- **Career fairs, info sessions and employer events.**
- **Funding** for unpaid or low-paid internships, at many schools.
- **Help with offers**, including evaluating and negotiating them.

The last two are especially underused. If you land an unpaid internship, your school may be able to fund it.

## When to go

Don't wait until you have an application due tomorrow. Good times to visit:

- **First year:** to explore interests and set up your Handshake and LinkedIn.
- **Before recruiting season** for your industry: to polish your resume and plan your timeline. See [internship recruiting timelines by industry](/guides/internship-recruiting-timeline).
- **Before any important interview**: for a mock interview.
- **When you're stuck**: no responses, no direction, or no motivation.
- **When you get an offer**: to understand it before you accept.

## How to prepare for an appointment

A focused 30-minute appointment beats a vague one. Before you go:

1. **Bring your resume**, printed or shared ahead of time.
2. **Bring a specific job posting** if you want help tailoring.
3. **Write down two or three questions** you want answered.
4. **Know your goal.** "I want to apply to marketing internships at consumer brands" is much easier to help with than "I need a job."

## Get the most out of each service

### Resume reviews

Ask the counselor to review your resume against a specific posting, not in general. Ask: "If you had 10 seconds, what would you notice first?" Then come back after revising.

### Mock interviews

These are among the most valuable things your career center offers. Ask for honest feedback, and if possible, record the session so you can watch yourself. Practice your STAR stories. See [STAR method examples for students](/guides/star-method-examples-for-students).

### Alumni connections

Ask directly: "Do you know any alumni in this field who like talking with students?" Career counselors often know which alumni are most responsive.

### Employer relationships

Career centers build relationships with employers who want students from your school. Ask which companies have hired students from your school recently, especially for internships.

## Build a relationship

Career counselors help hundreds of students. The ones who come back regularly, follow through on advice, and share updates tend to get remembered, and get mentioned when an employer calls asking for recommendations.

After a helpful appointment, send a quick thank-you, and update your counselor when something works out.

## What career centers can't do

Your career center can guide, review, and connect, but it can't do the applying for you. The work of finding roles, tailoring applications, and following up is still yours. Pair what you learn there with a steady weekly routine. See [how to job search during a busy semester](/guides/job-search-during-the-semester).

## Frequently asked questions

### What does a college career center do?

It helps students with resumes, cover letters, interview practice, career exploration, alumni connections, job databases, career fairs, and sometimes internship funding.

### When should I visit my career center?

Start in your first year, then visit before recruiting season, before important interviews, when you get an offer, and any time you're stuck.

### Is the career center worth it?

Yes, especially for mock interviews, resume reviews against specific postings, alumni connections and internship funding.

### Can my career center help with unpaid internships?

Many schools offer funding or stipends for unpaid or low-paid internships. Ask your career center what's available.
