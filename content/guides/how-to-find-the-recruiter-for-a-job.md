Applying online gets your resume into a system. Finding the recruiter gets it in front of a person. The difference between those two is often the difference between silence and an interview.

The good news is that recruiters aren't hiding. Their job is to be found by good candidates. You just need to know where to look.

## Start with the job posting itself

Read the full posting before you search anywhere else. Some companies name the recruiter or hiring manager directly, especially on LinkedIn, where postings often show "Meet the hiring team" with a name and profile.

Also look for:

- **A team or department name.** "Growth Marketing" or "Risk Analytics" tells you who the hiring manager probably is.
- **A location.** Larger companies often have recruiters by region or office.
- **A contact line** at the bottom, which sometimes includes a real person's email.

## Search LinkedIn the right way

LinkedIn is where most recruiters live professionally. Search for the company, open its **People** tab, and filter with keywords:

1. Search "recruiter," "talent acquisition," or "university recruiting." Many companies have someone dedicated to campus and internship hiring.
2. Narrow by location if the company is big.
3. For smaller companies with no recruiter at all, search the team name instead ("marketing," "engineering") and look for a manager or director.

If the company has a university recruiting team, that's usually the best person for internships. Their whole job is students like you.

## Check your school's alumni network

Alumni are the most underused resource in the job search. Search LinkedIn for the company plus your school's name, or use your school's alumni directory if it has one.

An alum may not be the recruiter, but they almost always know who is. And a message from a fellow student gets a much warmer reception than one from a stranger. We cover what to say in [how to email alumni for advice](/guides/how-to-email-alumni-for-career-advice).

## Look at the company's own site and social accounts

Recruiters often post about open roles on LinkedIn, X, or company blogs. Search the company's name plus "hiring" or "we're hiring interns." Career pages for smaller companies sometimes list a talent lead by name.

Company events are another source: campus info sessions, virtual career fairs, and webinars are usually run by the exact recruiters hiring for the roles being promoted.

## Figure out their email address

Once you have a name, you need an email. Most companies use one of a handful of formats:

- firstname@company.com
- firstname.lastname@company.com
- firstinitiallastname@company.com (for example, jsmith@)
- firstname_lastname@company.com

To find the right one:

1. **Look for any published email at the company**, such as a press contact or an author on the company blog. The format usually applies to everyone.
2. **Search the web for "@company.com"** in quotes to see real examples.
3. **Use an email-finding tool** if you're stuck. Several offer free lookups each month.

If you can't verify an address, sending a LinkedIn message instead is completely fine.

## When there's no recruiter at all

Startups and small companies often don't have recruiters. In that case, the hiring manager or even a founder is the right person. Look for the head of the team you'd join. At a company of 20 people, the founder may well read your email personally, which is a huge advantage over applying at a big company.

## What to do once you find them

Finding the right person is only half of it. You need to send something worth reading:

- **Apply through the official channel first.** Then reach out.
- **Keep it under 120 words**: the role, one strong reason you fit, and one easy ask.
- **Follow up once** if you don't hear back in about a week.

Our [follow-up guide](/guides/how-to-follow-up-on-a-job-application) has templates you can copy, and if you're writing before a role is even posted, see [cold emailing for internships](/guides/cold-email-for-internship-template).

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I contact the recruiter before or after I apply?

After, in almost every case. Recruiters need your application in their system before they can act on it. Mention in your email that you've already applied.

### Is it weird to message a recruiter on LinkedIn?

No. Messaging candidates and being messaged by candidates is a normal part of a recruiter's job. Keep it short, specific, and polite.

### What if I message the wrong person?

It happens and it's fine. People often forward messages to the right colleague, or reply with a name. Just thank them for their help.

### How do I find a recruiter's email for free?

Work out the company's email format from published addresses, search the web for examples of that format, and try LinkedIn messaging if you can't confirm an address.
