Almost every company you'll apply to uses an applicant tracking system, or ATS. Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, iCIMS, Taleo and others are the software behind most "Apply" buttons. Understanding what they do, and what they don't, can save you from a lot of bad advice.

## What an ATS actually does

An ATS is mostly a database and workflow tool for recruiters. When you apply, it:

1. **Parses your resume**, trying to pull out your name, contact information, education, jobs, dates and skills into structured fields.
2. **Stores your answers** to the application questions.
3. **Lets recruiters search and filter** candidates, by keyword, school, graduation year, location, work authorization or answers to specific questions.
4. **Tracks where each candidate is** in the process, from applied to interviewing to offer.

Some systems also rank or score candidates against the job description, and companies increasingly add AI screening tools on top.

## Myths to ignore

### "A robot rejects 75% of resumes automatically"

This statistic gets repeated everywhere, but it doesn't describe how most hiring works. Most of the time, the system doesn't auto-reject on resume content alone. The bigger risks are **knockout questions** (like work authorization or graduation date) and simply **not showing up** when a recruiter searches or sorts.

### "You need to stuff your resume with keywords"

Keyword stuffing, like pasting the job description in white text, doesn't fool modern systems and looks terrible to the human who eventually reads it. Use relevant keywords naturally and honestly.

### "Fancy resumes get thrown out"

Not thrown out exactly, but complex layouts can be parsed badly. If the system scrambles your work history, a recruiter searching for "Python" might never find you.

## How to make your resume parse cleanly

Here's a checklist that works for nearly every ATS:

- **Use a single-column layout.** Two columns can cause text to be read in the wrong order.
- **Use standard section headings**: Education, Experience, Projects, Skills, Leadership.
- **Avoid text boxes, tables, icons and graphics** for important information.
- **Put contact details in the body**, not only in the header or footer, which some parsers skip.
- **Use a standard font** like Calibri, Arial, Garamond or Helvetica.
- **Use consistent date formats**, like "May 2025 – Aug 2025."
- **Save as a PDF** unless the application specifically asks for Word. Make sure it's a text PDF, not a scanned image.

A quick test: open your PDF, select all, copy, and paste into a plain text file. If the text comes out in a sensible order, an ATS can probably read it.

## How to use keywords the right way

Recruiters search for skills and terms from the job description. To show up in those searches:

1. **Read the posting and highlight the skills, tools and phrases** that appear more than once or are listed as required.
2. **Include the ones you genuinely have**, using the same wording. If they say "Microsoft Excel," write "Microsoft Excel," not just "spreadsheets."
3. **Put keywords in context** in your bullets, not just in a skills list. "Built Excel models to forecast club budget" is better than "Excel" alone.
4. **Spell out acronyms once**: "Search Engine Optimization (SEO)."

## Watch out for knockout questions

The application form often matters more than the resume. Questions about these can filter you out instantly:

- Work authorization and sponsorship
- Graduation date and year in school
- Willingness to relocate or work in person
- Availability dates
- Minimum GPA

Read every question carefully and answer accurately. Knockout questions are one of the quiet reasons [applications disappear without a reply](/guides/why-no-response-to-job-applications). If you're unsure what a question means, check the job posting or ask the recruiter.

## Retyping the same information everywhere

One frustrating part of ATS-based applications is that many systems ask you to upload your resume and then retype the same information into form fields. It's tedious, and it's where typos and wrong dates sneak in. Double-check the auto-filled fields after you upload, because parsing errors are common.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is an applicant tracking system?

It's software companies use to collect, organize, search and track job applications. Workday, Greenhouse and Lever are common examples.

### Do applicant tracking systems reject resumes automatically?

Usually not based on resume content alone. Knockout questions, like work authorization or graduation date, are the more common automatic filters. Not appearing in recruiter searches is the bigger risk.

### Should my resume be a PDF or Word document for an ATS?

A text-based PDF works well with most modern systems. Use Word only if the application asks for it.

### How do I know if my resume is ATS-friendly?

Copy all the text from your PDF and paste it into a plain text editor. If it comes out in a clear, logical order, most systems will read it correctly.
