An informational interview is a short conversation with someone in a job, company or field you're curious about. You're not interviewing for a job; you're gathering information. Done well, it's one of the most effective things a student can do, because it builds real relationships with people inside the companies you want to join.

## Why informational interviews matter

In a 20-minute conversation, you can learn:

- What a job actually involves day to day.
- What skills and experiences the company really values.
- How hiring works on their team, and when roles open.
- Who else you should talk to.

And the person you talk to now knows your name, which matters a lot when an application comes across their desk.

## How to set one up

Reach out by email or LinkedIn with a short, specific request for 15 to 20 minutes. Alumni are the easiest place to start. Our guide to [emailing alumni for career advice](/guides/how-to-email-alumni-for-career-advice) has templates.

Once they agree:

- **Offer a few time options** and let them pick.
- **Ask whether they prefer phone or video.**
- **Send a calendar invite** with the call details.
- **Confirm the day before** with a quick, friendly note.

## How to prepare

Spend 15 to 20 minutes beforehand:

1. Read their LinkedIn profile carefully.
2. Look at the company's website and recent news.
3. Write down 5 to 7 questions you most want answered.
4. Prepare a 30-second introduction of yourself: year, major, interests, and what you're exploring.

## 15 great questions to ask

### About their path

1. How did you get started in this field?
2. What did you do in college that turned out to be most useful?
3. If you were a student again, what would you do differently?

### About the job

4. What does a typical week look like for you?
5. What's the most challenging part of your role?
6. What do you enjoy most about the work?
7. What skills matter most for success in this job?

### About the company

8. How would you describe the culture on your team?
9. How do interns or new hires usually get started?
10. What makes someone stand out when your team is hiring?

### About breaking in

11. What does the hiring process look like for internships here?
12. When do roles on your team usually open up?
13. Are there skills or experiences I should build before applying?
14. Are there resources, groups or events you'd recommend?

### The most important question

15. Is there anyone else you think I should talk to?

That last question is how one conversation turns into three.

## During the call

- **Be on time**, and keep to the time you asked for. If you asked for 15 minutes, offer to wrap up at 15.
- **Let them talk.** Listen more than you speak.
- **Take brief notes.**
- **Don't ask for a job.** If there's a role you're interested in, it's fine to mention it near the end: "I'm planning to apply for your summer internship. Is there anything you'd recommend I highlight?"

## After the call

Send a thank-you within 24 hours:

> Hi Jamie,
>
> Thank you so much for taking the time to talk today. Your advice about getting comfortable with SQL before recruiting season was really helpful, and I've already signed up for a course. I'll also reach out to Alex on the analytics team, as you suggested.
>
> I really appreciate it, and I hope we can stay in touch.
>
> Best,
> Riley

Then keep track of who you talked to and what you discussed. A few months later, send a short update about what you've done with their advice. People love hearing that their help mattered.

When you're ready to apply, a strong relationship may naturally lead to a referral. See [how to ask for a referral](/guides/how-to-ask-for-a-referral).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is an informational interview?

A short, informal conversation with someone in a job or company you're interested in, to learn about their work and get advice. It isn't a job interview.

### How long should an informational interview be?

Fifteen to 30 minutes. Ask for 15 or 20, and respect that time unless they offer to keep talking.

### Is it okay to ask for a job in an informational interview?

Don't ask for a job directly. It's fine to mention you're applying to a role and ask for advice on your application.

### What should I do after an informational interview?

Send a thank-you within 24 hours that mentions something specific you learned, follow their advice, and update them later on how it went.
