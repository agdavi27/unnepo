At large universities, dozens of big companies show up every fall. They hold info sessions, interview on campus, and fill much of their intern class from a short list of schools. If you go to a small liberal arts college or a smaller university, you might see a fraction of that.

That's a real disadvantage. But it's far from a dead end. Students from small schools land excellent internships every year. They just get there by a different route.

## Understand what you're up against

Many large employers focus campus recruiting on a list of "target schools." Students from other schools can still apply online, but they don't get the info sessions, the coffee chats or the on-campus interviews. Their applications go into the same online pile as everyone else's.

So the strategy is simple: **don't rely on the online pile.** Build the connections that target-school students get handed for free.

## Your hidden advantages

Small schools come with real strengths:

- **A tight alumni network.** Alumni from small schools are often especially loyal and responsive to current students.
- **Close relationships with professors**, who can write strong recommendations, connect you with former students, and offer research positions.
- **A career center that knows you by name.** Smaller staff, but often more personal attention.
- **A great story.** You stand out in a pile of applications from the same five big schools.

## Strategy 1: Make alumni your recruiting pipeline

Alumni are your version of on-campus recruiting. Search your school's LinkedIn alumni page for people at companies you're interested in, and reach out for short conversations.

At a small school, the message "I'm a current student at our school" carries real weight. Many alumni remember how hard it was to break in and want to help. See [how to email alumni for career advice](/guides/how-to-email-alumni-for-career-advice).

## Strategy 2: Go directly to recruiters and hiring managers

Since recruiters aren't coming to you, go to them. After applying online, find the recruiter or hiring manager and send a short, specific email. This is the step most students skip, and it matters even more when you're not from a target school.

See [how to find the recruiter for any job](/guides/how-to-find-the-recruiter-for-a-job).

## Strategy 3: Look beyond the obvious employers

The most famous companies get the most applicants and lean hardest on target schools. Expand your list:

- **Mid-size companies** that recruit more broadly.
- **Startups**, which care much more about what you can do than where you study.
- **Nonprofits, government offices and research organizations.**
- **Companies near your school or hometown**, where a local connection helps.

## Strategy 4: Use virtual and regional events

Many companies run virtual career fairs, diversity programs, and online info sessions open to all schools. Regional career fairs and consortiums, where several small colleges recruit together, are also worth attending. Ask your career center which ones your school participates in.

## Strategy 5: Build proof of your skills

When your school's name doesn't open doors automatically, your work can. Build things that show what you can do:

- A portfolio of projects, writing or design work.
- Research with a professor, especially if it leads to a presentation or paper.
- Leadership roles where you can point to concrete results.
- Certifications in tools employers use, like Excel, SQL or Google Analytics.

## Strategy 6: Start earlier and apply more broadly

Without campus recruiting to guide you, it's easy to miss deadlines. Learn the [recruiting timeline for your industry](/guides/internship-recruiting-timeline), and plan to send more applications than a target-school student might, while still tailoring each one.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I get a good internship from a small college?

Yes. Students from small colleges land strong internships every year, mostly through alumni connections, direct outreach to recruiters, and applying beyond the most famous companies.

### What is a target school?

A school where a company actively recruits, through info sessions, on-campus interviews and dedicated recruiters. Students from other schools can still apply, but have to build connections themselves.

### How do I network if my school doesn't have big career fairs?

Use your alumni network on LinkedIn, attend virtual and regional career events, and reach out directly to recruiters and hiring managers.

### Do employers care where I went to college?

Some do, especially in finance and consulting, but many employers care more about skills, experience and how you present yourself. Strong outreach can offset a lot.
