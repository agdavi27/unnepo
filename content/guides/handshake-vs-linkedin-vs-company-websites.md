The same internship can show up on Handshake, LinkedIn, Indeed and the company's own careers page. So where should you actually apply? And does it matter?

It does, a little. Here's how each platform works and how to use them together.

## Handshake

Handshake is built specifically for college students and is used by many career centers.

**Pros:**

- Employers on Handshake are often specifically looking for students.
- Many listings are from companies that have chosen to recruit at your school.
- Career fairs, info sessions and events are usually listed here.
- Some employers message students directly through the platform.

**Cons:**

- The selection of roles varies a lot by school.
- Some listings redirect to the company's site anyway.
- Applications can still disappear without a response.

**Best for:** finding employers interested in students from your school, and keeping up with campus events.

## LinkedIn

LinkedIn has a huge number of job postings and is also where most recruiters and alumni are.

**Pros:**

- Very broad selection of roles.
- You can see who posted a job, and often who's on the hiring team.
- Easy to find alumni and recruiters at the same company.
- Job alerts are flexible and useful.

**Cons:**

- "Easy Apply" postings can receive a flood of applicants.
- Some postings stay up after a role is filled.

**Best for:** discovering roles and, especially, finding the people behind them. See [how to find the recruiter for any job](/guides/how-to-find-the-recruiter-for-a-job).

## Indeed and other job boards

General job boards aggregate listings from all over the web.

**Pros:** wide coverage, including local and smaller employers that may not use Handshake.

**Cons:** more outdated postings, more irrelevant results, and occasional scams. See [how to spot job scams aimed at students](/guides/job-scams-targeting-college-students).

**Best for:** local, part-time and smaller-company roles.

## Company career websites

Most job boards ultimately send you to a company's own applicant tracking system.

**Pros:**

- Applying directly ensures your application lands in the company's system.
- Postings are usually the most up-to-date.
- You can see every open role at the company.

**Cons:**

- Every company's system is different, and you'll retype the same information over and over.
- You often need to create a separate account for each one.

**Best for:** your highest-priority applications.

## So where should you apply?

A good rule of thumb:

1. **Discover roles** on Handshake, LinkedIn and job boards. Set alerts on each.
2. **Apply through the company's own site** when you can, especially for roles you care about most. Some companies don't use one, and then the job board is fine.
3. **Find a person** on LinkedIn and follow up after applying. This is where the real advantage comes from. See our [follow-up templates](/guides/how-to-follow-up-on-a-job-application).
4. **Track everything in one place**, no matter where you applied. Applying across four platforms gets messy fast. See [how to track your job applications](/guides/how-to-track-job-applications).

## Does "Easy Apply" work?

Easy Apply is convenient, but convenience cuts both ways. Because it's so easy, those postings often get the most applicants. If you use it, treat it as the start: follow up with a recruiter or team member, just like any other application.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Handshake or LinkedIn better for internships?

Both are useful. Handshake is great for employers actively recruiting students at your school; LinkedIn has more listings and makes it easy to find recruiters and alumni.

### Should I apply on LinkedIn or the company website?

When possible, apply on the company's website, especially for roles you care about. Then use LinkedIn to find someone to follow up with.

### Does LinkedIn Easy Apply work for internships?

It can, but Easy Apply postings often attract many applicants. Following up with a recruiter or team member after applying improves your chances.

### Is Indeed good for college students?

It can be useful for local, part-time and smaller-company roles, but check postings carefully, since general job boards have more outdated listings and scams.
