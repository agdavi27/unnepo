When you've applied to four jobs, you can keep it all in your head. At fifteen, you start forgetting which ones you followed up on. At thirty, you get a call from a recruiter and can't remember which role you applied for.

Tracking your applications sounds boring, but it's what turns a chaotic job search into one you can actually manage.

## Why tracking matters

A good tracker helps you:

- **Follow up on time**, which is one of the biggest factors in getting a response.
- **Prepare for calls**, by knowing exactly which role and version of your resume you sent.
- **See what's working**, like which kinds of roles reply, and which don't.
- **Stay motivated**, because you can see progress even when replies are slow.

## The columns your tracker needs

Start with these:

1. **Company**
2. **Role**, the exact job title.
3. **Link** to the posting (postings often disappear after they close, so also save a PDF of the description).
4. **Date applied**
5. **Status**: Saved, Applied, Followed up, Interviewing, Offer, Rejected, No response.
6. **Contact**: the recruiter or person you reached out to, with their email.
7. **Next follow-up date**
8. **Resume version** you sent.
9. **Notes**: interview dates, who referred you, anything you discussed.

## A simple spreadsheet setup

Google Sheets or Excel works well. A few tricks make it far more useful:

- **Use a dropdown for Status** so it stays consistent and you can filter.
- **Add conditional formatting** so rows turn yellow when the follow-up date has passed.
- **Calculate the follow-up date automatically.** In Google Sheets, `=WORKDAY(D2, 5)` gives you five business days after the date in D2.
- **Freeze the header row** and sort by next follow-up date.
- **Add a second tab** for networking contacts, even ones not tied to a specific application.

## A weekly routine that keeps it working

A tracker only helps if you use it. Try a short weekly routine:

1. **Monday:** Sort by follow-up date and send any follow-ups that are due. Our [follow-up templates](/guides/how-to-follow-up-on-a-job-application) make this quick.
2. **During the week:** Add every new application the moment you submit it.
3. **Friday:** Update statuses, archive dead applications, and look at your numbers.

Fifteen minutes a week keeps everything from slipping.

## What your numbers can tell you

After 20 or 30 applications, look at your tracker:

- **Lots of applications, very few replies?** Revisit your resume and make sure you're applying early. See [why you never hear back](/guides/why-no-response-to-job-applications).
- **More replies from roles where you emailed someone?** That's your signal to reach out more.
- **Replies mostly from one type of company?** Consider focusing there.

## Spreadsheet vs. dedicated tools

Spreadsheets are free, flexible, and fine for many students. Their weaknesses show up as volume grows:

- **You have to type everything in manually**, which you'll eventually forget to do.
- **Reminders are passive.** A yellow row only helps if you open the sheet.
- **Contacts and emails live elsewhere**, so you're constantly switching tabs.

Dedicated tools save jobs from posting pages with a click, send follow-up reminders, and keep contacts attached to each application. If you're applying to more than 20 or 30 roles, the time saved usually adds up.

Whatever you use, the important thing is that you use it consistently.

## Frequently asked questions

### What's the best way to track job applications?

A simple spreadsheet with company, role, link, date applied, status, contact and next follow-up date works for most students. Dedicated tools help once you're applying to many roles.

### What columns should a job application tracker have?

At minimum: company, role, link, date applied, status, contact, next follow-up date and notes. Tracking your resume version helps too.

### How do I calculate follow-up dates in Google Sheets?

Use `=WORKDAY(start_date, 5)` to get the date five business days after you applied.

### How often should I update my tracker?

Add each application right after you submit it, and do a 15-minute review once a week to send follow-ups and update statuses.
