One of the most painful discoveries in college is learning that the internship you wanted closed months ago. Different industries recruit on wildly different schedules, and some start much earlier than students expect.

Exact dates shift every year and vary by company, so treat this as a map rather than a calendar. Always check the specific companies you care about.

## The big picture

Roughly speaking, industries fall into three groups:

- **Very early:** investment banking, some finance roles, and management consulting. Recruiting for a junior-year summer can begin the year before.
- **Fall:** big tech and many large corporations open applications in late summer and fall.
- **Winter and spring:** most other employers, including startups, nonprofits, media, small businesses and many government roles, hire closer to the summer.

## Industry by industry

### Investment banking and finance

Recruiting for junior-year summer analyst roles has moved earlier in recent years, with some firms opening applications and interviewing in the winter or spring of sophomore year. Networking often starts even before that.

If you're interested in finance, start learning about the industry and talking to alumni during your first and second years. Programs for first-years and sophomores, such as early insight weeks, can be stepping stones.

### Management consulting

Large consulting firms typically recruit juniors for summer internships in the late summer and fall, with applications often due early in the fall semester. Case interview prep takes weeks, so starting over the summer before junior year is common.

### Technology

Large tech companies often open internship applications in late summer through fall, and many roles are filled on a rolling basis, so applying early matters. Startups tend to hire later, often in winter and spring, and sometimes just a few weeks before summer.

### Government and policy

Federal internships often have application windows several months before the start date, and some require background checks that add time. State and local offices, think tanks and campaigns vary widely, and many hire in the spring.

### Nonprofits, media and the arts

These organizations usually hire in the spring, often with smaller teams and less formal processes. Direct outreach works especially well here. See [cold emailing for internships](/guides/cold-email-for-internship-template).

### Research

Formal summer research programs often have winter deadlines. For research with professors at your own school, ask in the fall or early spring.

## A month-by-month plan for a summer internship

### Summer before the school year

- Update your resume and LinkedIn.
- If you're targeting finance or consulting, start networking and prepping now.
- Make a list of target companies and note when each one recruits.

### September – November

- Apply to tech, consulting and large corporate programs as they open.
- Attend career fairs and info sessions.
- Start informational interviews with alumni. See [how to email alumni for advice](/guides/how-to-email-alumni-for-career-advice).

### December – February

- Keep applying as new roles post.
- Follow up on applications from the fall.
- Look at government and research programs with winter deadlines.

### March – May

- Apply to startups, nonprofits and small companies, which often hire now.
- Cold email organizations you'd like to work for, even without a posting.
- Don't panic if you don't have an offer yet. Many students land internships in April and May.

## If you've already missed the main window

It happens to almost everyone at some point. Options include:

- **Smaller companies and startups** that hire closer to summer.
- **Research with a professor** at your school.
- **Campus jobs** with real responsibility.
- **Personal projects** that build skills for next year.
- **Off-cycle internships** in the fall or spring semester.

Missing one cycle doesn't define your career. Use it to prepare earlier next time.

## Frequently asked questions

### When should I start applying for summer internships?

It depends on the industry. Finance and consulting can recruit the year before; large tech companies often open in late summer and fall; most other employers hire in winter and spring.

### Is it too late to apply for a summer internship in March?

No. Many startups, nonprofits, small businesses and research positions hire in March, April and even May.

### When do investment banking internships recruit?

For junior-year summer roles, recruiting has moved earlier, sometimes reaching into sophomore year. Check each firm's site and talk to alumni early.

### How do I keep track of all these deadlines?

Keep a list of target companies with their typical opening dates and set reminders. See [how to track your job applications](/guides/how-to-track-job-applications).
