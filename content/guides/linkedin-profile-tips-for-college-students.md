Recruiters look you up. Alumni look you up before replying to your message. Hiring managers look you up after reading your application. Your LinkedIn profile is often the second impression you make, right after your resume or email.

The good news is that a strong student profile takes about an hour to build. Here's exactly what to include.

## 1. A clear, friendly photo

Profiles with photos get much more engagement. You don't need a professional headshot:

- Use a recent photo where your face takes up most of the frame.
- Choose a plain or blurred background with good lighting, ideally natural light facing a window.
- Wear something you'd wear to an interview or a nice class presentation.
- Smile.

## 2. A headline that says more than "Student"

Your headline appears everywhere: in search results, comments and messages. Instead of "Student at Colby College," try:

- "Economics student at Colby · Interested in data analytics and fintech"
- "Computer Science @ Colby · Building web apps · Seeking summer 2027 SWE internship"
- "Biology major at Colby · Marine ecology research · Aspiring environmental consultant"

Include your school, your field, and what you're interested in or looking for.

## 3. A custom banner

The banner behind your photo is free space. A simple image related to your field, your school, or something you care about looks better than the default.

## 4. An About section in your own voice

Write three to five short sentences in first person:

> I'm a junior at Colby studying economics, and I'm most interested in how data can improve decisions for small businesses. I've built budgeting models for my campus club and analyzed dining hall traffic for a statistics project. I'm currently looking for a summer 2027 internship in analytics or strategy. Feel free to reach out!

Mention what you study, what you care about, a little proof, and what you're looking for.

## 5. Experience, including the "small" stuff

Add campus jobs, part-time work, research, leadership roles and internships. For each one:

- Use the official organization name so the logo appears.
- Add two or three bullet points, like on your resume.
- Lead with results. See [how to write a resume with no experience](/guides/resume-with-no-experience-college-student) for how to phrase things.

## 6. Education with details

Include your school, major, expected graduation date, and relevant coursework, honors, clubs or study abroad.

## 7. Skills that recruiters search for

Add skills that match the roles you want, such as Excel, Python, social media marketing, or public speaking. Recruiters search for these. Put your most relevant three at the top.

## 8. Projects and featured work

Use the **Projects** and **Featured** sections to show real work: a class presentation, a research poster, a website you built, an article you wrote. Showing beats telling.

## 9. A custom URL

Edit your public profile URL to something clean like linkedin.com/in/jordanlee. Put it on your resume and email signature.

## 10. Open to Work, set carefully

You can let recruiters know you're looking for internships, with the option to show it only to recruiters or to everyone. Specify the job titles and locations you're interested in so the right people find you.

## Using LinkedIn to find opportunities

A great profile is only half of it. Use LinkedIn actively:

- **Find alumni** at companies you're interested in through your school's Alumni page. See [how to email alumni](/guides/how-to-email-alumni-for-career-advice).
- **Find recruiters** in a company's People tab. See [how to find the recruiter for any job](/guides/how-to-find-the-recruiter-for-a-job).
- **Follow companies** you want to work for to see news and openings.
- **Set job alerts** for internships in your field.
- **Engage occasionally**: congratulate connections, comment thoughtfully, share something you learned.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do college students need a LinkedIn profile?

Yes, especially if you're looking for internships. Recruiters and alumni will look you up, and LinkedIn is one of the best tools for finding contacts at companies.

### What should a college student put as a LinkedIn headline?

Your school, your field of study, and what you're interested in or looking for, such as "Economics student at Colby · Interested in data analytics."

### Should I include part-time jobs on LinkedIn?

Yes. Campus and part-time jobs show reliability, communication and responsibility, especially early in college.

### How do I write a LinkedIn About section as a student?

Write three to five sentences in first person covering what you study, what interests you, a bit of evidence, and what you're looking for.
