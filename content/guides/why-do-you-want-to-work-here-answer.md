It shows up on applications, in phone screens, and in final rounds: "Why do you want to work here?" It sounds easy, which is exactly why so many students answer it badly. "It's a great company with a great culture" tells the interviewer nothing except that you didn't prepare.

Here's how to give an answer that actually sets you apart.

## What the interviewer is really asking

Behind the question are three things they want to know:

1. **Did you do your homework?** Do you understand what this company actually does?
2. **Are you genuinely interested**, or is this one of 80 identical applications?
3. **Will you be a good fit?** Do your interests and goals line up with the role?

A strong answer covers all three in about 45 to 60 seconds, or three to five sentences in writing.

## The three-part formula

### 1. Something specific about the company

Name a product, project, mission, recent announcement, or way the company works that genuinely interests you. The more specific, the better.

### 2. A connection to you

Link that specific thing to your experience, studies or interests. This is what makes the answer yours and not something any candidate could say.

### 3. What you'd contribute or learn in this role

Bring it back to the job. Show that you understand what the role does and why you'd be good at it.

## How to research in 15 minutes

You don't need hours. Before writing your answer:

- **Read the company's About page and recent news** or blog posts.
- **Look at the product or service yourself.** Use the app, read their reports, visit the store.
- **Read the job description again**, especially the responsibilities.
- **Look up two or three employees on LinkedIn**, ideally people on the team you'd join. What do they work on?
- **Talk to an alum** if you can. One conversation gives you material nobody else has. See [informational interview questions to ask](/guides/informational-interview-questions).

## Example answers

### Marketing internship at an outdoor gear company

> "I've been a customer since high school, but what really got my attention was your repair program, where you fix gear instead of replacing it. I lead trips for our campus outdoors club, so I see firsthand how much people care about gear lasting. I'd love to help tell that story to college-aged customers, and I've been running the club's Instagram, so I already have a feel for what content gets students to engage."

### Data analyst internship at a healthcare nonprofit

> "Your work tracking vaccination access in rural counties is exactly the kind of problem I want to work on. I grew up in a rural area, and in my statistics coursework I did a project mapping clinic distances across my home state. This internship would let me use those skills on data that actually changes decisions, and I'd learn far more from your team than I could in a classroom."

### Software engineering internship at a fintech startup

> "I've used your budgeting app for a year, and I really like how you handle shared expenses, which most apps get wrong. As a small team, you give interns real ownership, and I want to ship code people actually use. I built a bill-splitting tool for my apartment last semester, so I'd come in already thinking about the problems you're solving."

## Mistakes that make answers sound generic

- **Talking only about prestige or pay.** "You're a top firm" is about you, not them.
- **Praising "the culture"** without saying what you mean.
- **Reciting the mission statement.** Show what it means to you instead.
- **Making it all about what you'll get.** Balance learning with what you'd contribute.
- **Going too long.** Two minutes of rambling is worse than 45 seconds of focus.

## Writing it for an application

When this question appears in a written application, the same formula works in three to five sentences. Avoid starting with "I want to work at your company because." Open with the specific detail instead. For more on written questions, see [how to write strong short answers on applications](/guides/how-to-answer-short-answer-application-questions).

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should my answer to "Why do you want to work here?" be?

About 45 to 60 seconds when spoken, or three to five sentences in writing.

### What should I avoid when answering "Why this company?"

Avoid generic praise, salary or prestige as your main reason, reciting the mission statement, and answers that could apply to any company.

### How do I answer if I don't know much about the company?

Spend 15 minutes researching their products, recent news and the job description before you answer. Even one specific detail makes a big difference.

### Is it okay to say I want to learn?

Yes, but pair it with what you'd contribute. Companies want interns who are eager to learn and will also add value.
