A referral is when someone inside a company recommends you for a role. It's one of the most effective ways to get your application noticed, because recruiters often treat referred candidates as higher priority.

Many students are nervous about asking for referrals. Done respectfully, though, it's a normal part of how hiring works, and many companies actively encourage employees to refer people.

## Why referrals work

- **Recruiters pay attention.** Many applicant tracking systems flag referred candidates, and referred applications are more likely to be reviewed by a person.
- **Trust transfers.** A recommendation from an employee signals that you're worth a look.
- **Employees are often rewarded.** Some companies pay referral bonuses, so you may be helping them too.

## Who to ask

In rough order of how natural the ask is:

1. **Friends and classmates** who work or have interned at the company.
2. **Alumni** you've already talked with. See [how to email alumni for career advice](/guides/how-to-email-alumni-for-career-advice).
3. **Former managers, coworkers and professors** with connections at the company.
4. **People you've met** at career fairs, info sessions or [informational interviews](/guides/informational-interview-questions).
5. **Cold contacts**, which is possible but harder. Build a little rapport first.

The best referrers know your work, or at least have had a real conversation with you.

## When to ask

- **Before you apply**, ideally. Some companies require the referral to come first, because the employee submits your information through an internal system.
- **After you've built some connection.** Asking a stranger for a referral in your first message rarely works. A short conversation first makes a big difference.
- **Early in the hiring cycle**, before the role fills.

## How to make it easy for them

The easier you make it, the more likely they'll say yes. Send:

- **The link to the specific role** (or the job ID).
- **Your resume.**
- **Two or three sentences** on why you're a good fit, which they can paste into the referral form.
- **A graceful out**, so they don't feel pressured.

## Template: Asking an alum you've already talked to

> **Subject:** Quick favor: referral for the Analyst Internship?
>
> Hi Jamie,
>
> Thanks again for talking with me last month. Your advice about learning SQL was a big help, and I've been working through a course since.
>
> I'm applying for the Summer Analyst Internship on the strategy team (Job ID 12345). Would you be comfortable referring me? I've attached my resume, and here's a short summary in case it's helpful: I'm a junior economics major at Colby, I've built budget models for a $4,000 club budget, and I recently analyzed dining hall data to help adjust staffing.
>
> No pressure at all if it's not a good fit. I really appreciate your help either way.
>
> Best,
> Riley Chen

## Template: Asking a friend or classmate

> Hey Sam! I saw that Brightline is hiring a marketing intern for the summer, and I'm planning to apply. Would you be up for referring me? I can send you my resume and the job link. Totally fine if not!

## Template: Asking someone you just met

If you've just had a good conversation, ask for advice first, and let a referral come up naturally:

> Thanks so much for talking with me today. I'm planning to apply for the internship on your team. Would you recommend I apply directly, or is there a better way to get my application in front of the right person?

Often, they'll offer to refer you.

## After you're referred

- **Apply promptly** if you haven't already, and mention the referral where the application asks.
- **Thank them right away.**
- **Keep them updated**, whether you get an interview or not.
- **Send a thank-you note** if you get the job, and offer to return the favor.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it rude to ask for a referral?

No, as long as you ask respectfully and give the person an easy way to say no. Referrals are a normal part of hiring, and many companies encourage them.

### How do I ask for a referral from someone I don't know well?

Have a short conversation first, such as an informational interview. Then ask for advice on the best way to apply, which often leads to a referral offer.

### Should I ask for a referral before or after applying?

Before, if possible. At many companies, the employee submits the referral through an internal system, which may create or link your application.

### What should I send someone who's referring me?

The job link or ID, your resume, and a two- or three-sentence summary of why you're a good fit that they can paste into the referral form.
