At many companies, the summer internship is the main pipeline for full-time hiring. Doing well can mean walking into senior year with a job offer already in hand, which takes enormous pressure off your final year.

Return offers aren't only about being the smartest intern. They're about being reliable, easy to work with, and clearly interested. Here's how to show all three.

## What managers actually look for

When managers discuss return offers, they tend to ask:

- **Did they do good work?** Reliable, accurate, and on time.
- **Did they take ownership?** Did they drive their project forward, or wait to be told what to do?
- **Were they easy to work with?** Positive, open to feedback, respectful of people's time.
- **Did they learn quickly?** Did they improve over the summer?
- **Do they want to be here?** Interest and enthusiasm matter.

Notice that most of these are about behavior, not raw talent.

## Weeks 1–2: Start strong

- **Clarify expectations.** In your first week, ask your manager: "What would make this internship a success, from your perspective?" Write down the answer.
- **Learn names and roles.** Take notes on who does what.
- **Ask good questions**, and write down the answers so you don't ask twice.
- **Set up a regular check-in** with your manager, even 15 minutes a week.

## Weeks 3–6: Do excellent work and build relationships

- **Own your project.** Track your own deadlines, flag problems early, and propose solutions, not just questions.
- **Communicate proactively.** A short weekly update to your manager ("Here's what I finished, what I'm working on, and where I'm stuck") makes you easy to manage.
- **Ask for feedback mid-summer**, and act on it visibly.
- **Meet people beyond your team.** Ask for 20-minute coffee chats with people in other roles. Many of the questions in our [informational interview guide](/guides/informational-interview-questions) work well here.

## Weeks 7–10: Finish well and make your interest clear

- **Deliver a polished final presentation.** Rehearse it. Explain the problem, what you did, the results, and what's next.
- **Document your work** so the team can use it after you leave.
- **Tell your manager you'd like to come back.** Many interns assume their interest is obvious. Say it directly: "I've really enjoyed this summer, and I'd love to return full-time. Is there anything I should focus on to make that possible?"
- **Ask about the timeline** for return offers so you're not left guessing.

## Small things that make a big difference

- **Be on time**, to work and to meetings.
- **Respond to messages promptly**, even just to say you'll look into it.
- **Be careful with details.** Typos in an email to a director are memorable for the wrong reasons.
- **Participate in intern events** and social activities.
- **Say thank you** to the people who helped you.

## After the internship

- **Send thank-you notes** to your manager, mentor and anyone who helped, mentioning something specific. See [thank-you email templates](/guides/thank-you-email-after-interview) for the tone.
- **Connect on LinkedIn** with the people you worked with.
- **Update your resume** while your accomplishments are fresh, with numbers.
- **Stay in touch** through the fall, especially if offers come later.

## If you don't get a return offer

It happens, often for reasons unrelated to your performance, like budget changes or limited headcount. Ask your manager for a reference and feedback, keep the relationships, and use your new experience to strengthen your full-time search. See [what to do after a rejection](/guides/what-to-do-after-a-job-rejection).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a return offer?

An offer to come back to the same company after your internship, either for another internship or a full-time job after graduation.

### How do interns get return offers?

By doing reliable work, taking ownership of their projects, being easy to work with, improving over the summer, and clearly expressing interest in returning.

### Should I tell my manager I want a return offer?

Yes. Say it directly toward the end of the internship, and ask what you should focus on to make it possible.

### What if I don't get a return offer?

Ask for feedback and a reference, stay in touch, and use your internship experience to strengthen your applications elsewhere.
