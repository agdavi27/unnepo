Every student hits the same wall: internships want experience, and you need an internship to get experience. The good news is that "no experience" almost never means nothing to put on a resume. It means you haven't learned how to describe what you've already done.

Here's how to build a resume that gets interviews, even if you've never had a professional job.

## What counts as experience

Recruiters hiring interns know you're a student. They aren't expecting a career. They're looking for evidence that you can learn, follow through, and work with other people. That evidence can come from almost anywhere:

- **Class projects**, especially group projects, research papers, and anything with a real deliverable.
- **Campus jobs**: library desk, dining hall, tour guide, tutor, residential advisor.
- **Clubs and organizations**, particularly any leadership role or anything you organized.
- **Part-time and summer jobs**: retail, food service, camp counselor, lifeguard, babysitting.
- **Volunteering** and community service.
- **Personal projects**: a website, an app, a podcast, a small business, a fundraiser.
- **Athletics**, which show discipline, time management, and teamwork.

A shift supervisor at a coffee shop handled cash, trained new hires and dealt with unhappy customers. That's real, transferable experience.

## The sections to include

Keep it to one page. Use this order:

1. **Header:** name, email, phone, city, and a LinkedIn link.
2. **Education:** school, degree, major, expected graduation date, GPA if it's 3.3 or above, and relevant coursework.
3. **Experience:** jobs, internships, research, and significant roles.
4. **Projects:** academic or personal projects with real outcomes.
5. **Leadership and activities:** clubs, sports, volunteering.
6. **Skills:** software, languages, tools, technical skills.

With little work experience, Education goes at the top. Once you have a relevant internship, Experience moves up.

## How to write bullet points that impress

Every bullet should follow a simple pattern: **action verb + what you did + result**.

Compare these:

- Weak: "Responsible for club social media."
- Strong: "Grew the club's Instagram from 300 to 1,100 followers in one semester by posting weekly event recaps."

- Weak: "Worked at the campus library."
- Strong: "Helped about 40 students a shift find research materials and trained three new desk assistants."

- Weak: "Did a group project on renewable energy."
- Strong: "Led a four-person team analyzing solar adoption across 50 states; presented findings to a class of 60."

Numbers are your best friend. Estimate honestly if you don't have exact figures: "about 40," "roughly 200," "three new hires."

## Strong action verbs to start bullets

Avoid "helped with" and "responsible for." Use specific verbs instead:

- **Leadership:** led, organized, coordinated, launched, managed
- **Analysis:** analyzed, researched, measured, evaluated, identified
- **Communication:** presented, wrote, pitched, trained, explained
- **Building:** built, designed, created, developed, improved
- **Results:** increased, reduced, raised, saved, grew

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Going over one page.** As a student, one page is always enough.
- **Using a fancy template.** Two-column designs, icons and graphics often break when an applicant tracking system reads them. Simple wins. See [how applicant tracking systems read your resume](/guides/how-applicant-tracking-systems-read-resumes).
- **Including high school details** after your first year of college, unless they're truly exceptional.
- **Listing soft skills** like "hard-working" or "team player." Show them in your bullets instead.
- **Typos.** Read it out loud, then have someone else read it.

## Frequently asked questions

### What do I put on a resume if I have no experience?

Class projects, campus jobs, clubs, volunteering, sports and personal projects all count. Describe each with an action verb, what you did, and a result.

### Should I include my GPA on my resume?

Include it if it's around 3.3 or higher, or if the application asks for it. If it's lower, you can leave it off and let your experience speak.

### Should a college student's resume be one page?

Yes. Recruiters spend only a few seconds on a first pass, and one page forces you to keep only your strongest material.

### Can I include high school activities on a college resume?

In your first year, yes, if they're relevant or impressive. By sophomore or junior year, replace them with college experience.
