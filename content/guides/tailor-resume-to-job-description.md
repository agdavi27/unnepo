Everyone says you should tailor your resume for every job. Almost nobody explains how to do it without spending an hour per application. Here's a ten-minute process that gets most of the benefit with a fraction of the effort.

## Why tailoring matters

A recruiter skimming your resume is asking one question: does this person look like what we need? A tailored resume makes that answer obvious in seconds. It also helps you show up when recruiters search their applicant tracking system for specific skills. (More on that in [how applicant tracking systems read your resume](/guides/how-applicant-tracking-systems-read-resumes).)

You're not inventing anything. You're choosing which true things to emphasize.

## Before you start: build a master resume

Tailoring is fast only if you have something to cut from. Create a master resume, which can run two or three pages, with:

- Every job, project, club role and volunteer experience you've had.
- Several bullet variations for each, emphasizing different skills.
- A full list of your technical skills, tools and coursework.

You'll never send this version. It's your raw material.

## The 10-minute process

### Minutes 1–3: Read the posting and pull out keywords

Read the job description once all the way through. Then go back and highlight:

- **Required skills and tools** ("Excel," "Python," "social media analytics").
- **Repeated phrases**: anything mentioned two or more times is important.
- **The core responsibilities**: what will you actually spend your days doing?

Write down the top five to eight terms.

### Minutes 3–5: Reorder your sections and experiences

Move the most relevant experience to the top. If you're applying to a data role and your best data work is a class project, move Projects above Experience. Put the most relevant bullet first under each role.

### Minutes 5–8: Swap in matching bullets and wording

Go through your bullets and:

- **Swap in versions from your master resume** that highlight the skills in the posting.
- **Use the posting's language** where it's accurate. If they say "stakeholder communication," and you presented to a client, write "presented findings to client stakeholders."
- **Cut bullets that don't support this role** to keep everything to one page.

### Minutes 8–10: Update your skills and proofread

Make sure the tools and skills from the posting that you genuinely have appear in your Skills section. Then save it with a clear name, such as "Jordan_Lee_Resume_Brightline.pdf," and give it one last read for typos.

## An example

Imagine the posting is for a marketing internship and mentions "content creation," "Instagram and TikTok," "analytics," and "Canva" several times.

Before tailoring, a bullet might read:

> Ran social media for the campus outdoors club.

After tailoring:

> Created weekly Instagram and TikTok content in Canva for the outdoors club, using post analytics to double average engagement over one semester.

Same experience, but now it directly answers what the recruiter is looking for.

## What not to do

- **Don't add skills you don't have.** You'll be asked about them in the interview.
- **Don't copy the posting word for word.** Mirror the key terms, not entire sentences.
- **Don't change your job titles** to match theirs. Keep titles accurate, and let your bullets do the matching.
- **Don't forget the application questions.** Tailoring your resume doesn't help if your short answers are generic. See [how to answer short-answer application questions](/guides/how-to-answer-short-answer-application-questions).

## How much tailoring is enough?

Not every application needs the full ten minutes. A good rule:

- **Dream roles:** full tailoring, a custom cover letter, and a follow-up email to a real person.
- **Good-fit roles:** reorder sections, swap two or three bullets, update your skills.
- **Quick applications:** at minimum, make sure your top three bullets match the role.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I really need to tailor my resume for every job?

You don't need to rewrite it, but adjusting the order, top bullets and skills for each role noticeably improves how well your resume matches what recruiters search for and skim.

### How do I tailor my resume quickly?

Keep a master resume with every experience and several bullet versions. For each job, pull the key terms from the posting, reorder sections, and swap in the bullets that match.

### Is it okay to use the exact words from the job description?

Yes, for skills and key terms you genuinely have. Avoid copying whole sentences, and never claim skills you don't have.

### Should I change my resume's file name for each job?

A clear file name like "FirstName_LastName_Resume.pdf" is all you need. Adding the company name helps you keep versions organized.
