Many big internship programs are designed for juniors. If you're a first-year or sophomore, it can feel like nothing is open to you yet. But the summers before junior year matter a lot: they're where you build the experience that makes junior-year applications competitive.

The good news is there are more options than it seems. You just have to look in different places.

## Programs built for early students

Some large companies run programs specifically for first-years and sophomores, sometimes called early insight programs, sophomore internships, or exploration programs. They're common in finance, consulting and tech, and often focus on students from underrepresented backgrounds.

These programs are competitive and often recruit early, so search for them in the fall. Your career center can point you to ones that have hired students from your school.

## Smaller companies and startups

Small companies care less about your year and more about whether you can help. A sophomore who can manage social media, build a simple website, or analyze a spreadsheet is valuable to a 15-person company.

Startups and small businesses often don't post internships at all. Reach out directly. See [cold emailing for internships](/guides/cold-email-for-internship-template).

## Research with professors

Research is one of the best early experiences you can get. It teaches real skills, leads to strong recommendations, and looks great on applications in almost any field.

- **Ask professors whose classes you enjoyed.** Go to office hours and express interest.
- **Check your school's summer research programs**, which often fund students.
- **Look for formal research programs** at other universities, which typically have winter deadlines.

## Nonprofits, government and local organizations

Local nonprofits, government offices, museums, hospitals and community organizations often welcome student help. These roles build real skills like writing, project management, outreach and data work, and often lead to great references.

## Campus jobs with real responsibility

Not every valuable experience has "intern" in the title. Campus jobs like research assistant, writing tutor, admissions tour guide, IT help desk or communications assistant build skills employers value. Leadership positions in clubs count too.

## Build something yourself

If you can't find a formal role, create your own experience:

- Start a small business, newsletter or podcast.
- Build an app or website.
- Run a fundraiser or organize an event.
- Do freelance work for local businesses.
- Complete a substantial independent project in your field.

A personal project with real results often impresses recruiters more than a vague internship where you mostly made copies.

## Make your early experience count

Whatever you do, focus on results you can describe later:

- **Track what you accomplish**, with numbers when possible.
- **Ask for feedback** and a reference at the end.
- **Update your resume and LinkedIn** right away. See [how to write a resume with no experience](/guides/resume-with-no-experience-college-student).

## Start building relationships now

The first two years are also the perfect time to start talking to alumni. You're not asking for a job, just learning, which makes conversations relaxed and genuine. These connections can turn into referrals when you're applying as a junior. See [how to email alumni for career advice](/guides/how-to-email-alumni-for-career-advice).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can freshmen get internships?

Yes. Options include early insight and sophomore programs at large companies, internships at small companies and startups, research with professors, nonprofits, and campus jobs with real responsibility.

### What should a sophomore do in the summer if they can't find an internship?

Research with a professor, a local nonprofit or government role, a campus job, freelance work, or a substantial personal project all build valuable experience.

### Do companies hire sophomores for internships?

Some do, especially small companies, startups and organizations with dedicated sophomore programs. Many large programs focus on juniors.

### When should freshmen and sophomores apply for internships?

Early programs at large companies often recruit in the fall. Small companies, nonprofits and research positions usually hire in winter and spring.
