Staring at a blank cover letter is hard enough. Staring at one when you feel like you have nothing to say is worse. But a cover letter for an internship isn't supposed to list professional accomplishments. It's supposed to show that you understand the role, that you want it for real reasons, and that you'd be good to work with.

You can do all three without a single prior internship.

## What a recruiter wants from an intern's cover letter

Recruiters hiring interns read cover letters looking for:

- **Genuine interest** in this company and this role, not a generic letter with the name swapped in.
- **Evidence you can do the basics**: communicate clearly, learn quickly, and follow through.
- **One or two concrete stories** that hint at what you'd bring.
- **Clear, error-free writing.** A cover letter is also a writing sample.

## The four-paragraph structure

Keep it under one page, about 250 to 350 words.

### Paragraph 1: The hook

Say what role you're applying for and give one specific reason you're drawn to this company. Skip "I am writing to express my interest." Start with something real.

### Paragraph 2: Your strongest story

Pick one experience from class, a campus job, a club, a sport, or a personal project, and tell it briefly. What was the situation, what did you do, and what happened? Connect it to a skill the role needs.

### Paragraph 3: Why this role fits you

Show that you understand what the internship involves and explain why you're a fit. Mention a second skill or experience, or what you hope to learn and contribute.

### Paragraph 4: The close

Thank them, restate your interest in one line, and say you'd welcome the chance to talk.

## A full example

> Dear Hiring Team,
>
> When my campus food pantry ran out of stock two weeks into the semester, I realized we had no idea which items students actually needed. That's why the Data Analyst Internship at Harvest Network caught my attention: your work using data to reduce food waste across regional pantries is exactly the kind of problem I want to spend a summer on.
>
> As a volunteer coordinator for the pantry, I built a simple Google Sheets tracker for donations and pickups. After a month of data, I found that three items made up nearly half of all requests, and we changed our donation drive to focus on them. Shortages dropped noticeably that semester, and the tracker is still in use.
>
> I'm a sophomore studying economics with coursework in statistics and data analysis in R. I'm comfortable cleaning messy data and explaining what it means to people who don't love spreadsheets. I'm eager to learn how a larger organization turns data into decisions, and I'd bring the same curiosity and follow-through I've brought to the pantry.
>
> Thank you for considering my application. I'd welcome the chance to talk about how I could contribute to Harvest Network this summer.
>
> Sincerely,
> Taylor Morgan

Notice that the student never apologizes for their lack of experience. They lead with a real story and connect it directly to the role.

## Phrases to avoid

- **"I know I don't have much experience, but..."** Never apologize. Lead with what you have.
- **"I am a hard-working team player."** Show it with a story instead.
- **"I am writing to apply for..."** Your reader already knows that.
- **"To whom it may concern."** Use a name if you can find one, or "Dear Hiring Team."
- **Repeating your resume line by line.** The cover letter should add context, not duplicate.

## Tips that make a big difference

- **Find a name.** Addressing the letter to a real person shows effort. See [how to find the recruiter for any job](/guides/how-to-find-the-recruiter-for-a-job).
- **Mention something specific** about the company: a product, a project, a value, a recent announcement.
- **Match the tone of the company.** A startup might welcome a slightly more casual voice; a bank probably won't.
- **Proofread out loud.** You'll catch awkward sentences your eyes skip over.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I write a cover letter for an internship with no experience?

Lead with genuine interest in the company, tell one concrete story from class, a campus job, a club or a project, and connect it to the skills the role needs. Never apologize for a lack of experience.

### How long should an internship cover letter be?

About 250 to 350 words, comfortably under one page. Recruiters skim, so shorter and specific beats longer and generic.

### Who should I address an internship cover letter to?

The recruiter or hiring manager by name if you can find them. If not, "Dear Hiring Team" is a safe, professional choice.

### Can I use the same cover letter for every internship?

You can reuse your structure and stories, but rewrite the opening and the "why this company" parts for each application. Generic letters are easy to spot.
