AI tools can write a cover letter in ten seconds. That's exactly the problem: so can everyone else's. Recruiters now see stacks of applications that all sound the same, full of words like "passionate," "leverage" and "thrilled to apply."

Used well, AI can save you hours and make your applications better. Used badly, it makes you sound like everyone else. Here's how to stay on the right side of that line.

## Where AI genuinely helps

- **Getting past the blank page.** A rough first draft you then rewrite is far easier than starting from nothing.
- **Tailoring at speed.** Comparing your resume to a job description and suggesting which experiences to emphasize.
- **Editing.** Tightening wordy sentences, catching typos, and cutting a 400-word answer down to 200.
- **Brainstorming.** Generating possible STAR stories from your experiences, or questions to ask in an interview.
- **Interview practice.** Asking it to play an interviewer and give feedback on your answers. Pair it with [the STAR method](/guides/star-method-examples-for-students).
- **The repetitive parts.** Filling in the same contact details, education and work history across dozens of applications.

## Where it backfires

- **Inventing experience.** Never let AI add skills, numbers or accomplishments you don't have. You'll be asked about them.
- **Generic voice.** Unedited AI writing tends to be vague, over-enthusiastic and full of clichés. Recruiters notice.
- **Factual errors.** AI can get company details wrong. Always verify anything specific about the company.
- **Ignoring instructions.** Some applications ask you not to use AI for certain questions. Respect that.

## A practical workflow

### 1. Start with your real material

Give the tool your actual resume, the job description, and a few notes in your own words about why you want the role. The more real detail you provide, the less generic the output.

### 2. Ask for structure, not polish

Prompts like "Suggest which three of my experiences best fit this role and why" or "Outline a cover letter using my food pantry story" produce more useful results than "Write me a cover letter."

### 3. Rewrite in your own voice

Read the draft out loud. Anything you wouldn't actually say, change. Replace generic phrases with specific details: names, numbers, what actually happened.

### 4. Cut the AI tells

Watch for words and phrases that show up everywhere in AI writing, such as "I am thrilled," "passionate about leveraging," "in today's fast-paced world," "I am confident that my skills," and "delve." Delete them or replace them with plain language.

### 5. Verify and proofread

Check every fact about the company, and make sure every claim about you is true.

## Before and after

AI draft:

> I am thrilled to apply for this exciting opportunity. As a passionate and detail-oriented student, I am confident that my skills would make me an invaluable asset to your dynamic team.

Rewritten:

> I run the budget for my campus economics club, which means I spend a lot of time making messy spreadsheets readable. That's why your analyst internship, with its focus on cleaning and visualizing sales data, caught my eye.

The second version is shorter, specific and sounds like a real person.

## Is using AI to apply for jobs cheating?

Using AI to draft, edit and save time is broadly accepted, much like using spell-check or asking a friend to review your resume. What matters is that the content is true and the final words are yours. Follow any specific instructions on an application, and be ready to speak to anything you submit.

## Use AI for the busywork, and your time for people

The best use of AI is handling the repetitive parts of applying, so you have more time for the parts that actually move the needle: tailoring your strongest applications and reaching out to real people. A follow-up email to a recruiter written in your own voice is worth more than any perfectly generated cover letter. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is it okay to use AI to write a cover letter?

Using AI to draft or edit is common, but rewrite the result in your own voice with specific, true details. Unedited AI cover letters tend to sound generic.

### Can recruiters tell if you used AI?

Often they can tell when writing is generic and full of common AI phrases. Specific details and a natural voice make AI-assisted writing indistinguishable from your own, because it is your own.

### What's the best way to use AI in a job search?

Use it for first drafts, tailoring suggestions, editing, interview practice and filling in repetitive information. Spend the time you save on networking and follow-ups.

### Should I let AI fill out job applications?

Tools that fill in your basic details save a lot of time, as long as you review every answer before submitting. Never let any tool submit something you haven't read.
