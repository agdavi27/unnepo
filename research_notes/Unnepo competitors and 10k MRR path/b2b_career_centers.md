# Selling Student Career Tools to Colleges, Bootcamps and Student Orgs (B2B/B2B2C), as of late 2026

Research note: about 22 searches and fetches. Vendors almost never publish institutional prices. The hard numbers below come from public student technology fee records (University of Washington, CUNY John Jay), a GSA federal schedule, and a few vendor pages. Where a number comes from a vendor or competitor blog, I flag it.

## 1. What career centers buy and what they pay

### Takeaway
Career centers buy (a) a core career services management (CSM) platform: Handshake, Symplicity or 12twenty; (b) bolt-on AI tools: resume review (VMock, Quinncia, Hiration, Rezi), interview practice (Big Interview, Yoodli) and content libraries (Vault/Firsthand, CareerShift); (c) niche tools such as Interstride for international students. Public contract figures cluster at about $1k–$27k per year per tool at a single school. Multi-campus flagship deals reach about $100k+. Handshake's own university fee is roughly $8–10k per year.

### Cited Findings
**Handshake (CSM platform)**
- A University of Washington document lists Handshake's total cost at about $10,000 (plus tax) per year. Sacra reports universities "typically paid around $8,000 per year" and describes Handshake as under $10K per year, versus older campus software. These figures come from search summaries. The UW PDF itself was image-only and its text could not be read. — [UW Tacoma 26A0529 Handshake platform PDF (Jan 2026)](https://www.tacoma.uw.edu/sites/default/files/2026-01/26a0529-handshake-platform.pdf); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/2f92db9f-5d2a-4814-a0df-aef6277059b0/)
- Handshake charges employers separately: Pro is $450/month or $4,500/year. — [Handshake support](https://support.joinhandshake.com/hc/en-us/articles/115014792267-How-Much-Does-Handshake-Cost)
- Symplicity powers Purdue's "Purdue Career Gateway" all-in-one platform (the announcement is undated). — [Purdue CS announcement](https://cs.purdue.edu/corporate/announcements/cco_careergateway.html)

**VMock (AI resume, pitch and LinkedIn feedback)**
- CUNY John Jay College (about 15,000 students): annual VMock renewal of **$25,000 recurring**. Submitted 2026-04-28 and approved 2026-08-11, funded through the student tech fee. The justification: the Career Learning Lab has 6 staff, and VMock "expands CLL staff's reach." An earlier John Jay proposal listed $27,000 recurring. — [John Jay Tech Fee project 418](https://doitapps2.jjay.cuny.edu/techfee/project_detail.php?project_id=418); [project 376](https://doitapps2.jjay.cuny.edu/techfee/project_detail.php?project_id=376)
- University of Washington, 3 campuses: **$115,763** requested and fully awarded for VMock SMART Resume, Elevator Pitch and Aspire (LinkedIn). The year is not stated. — [UW Tech Fee proposal](https://techfee.uw.edu/proposal/vmock-smart-career-platform-uniform-access-block/)
- **Churn signal:** Bentley's career center announced in October 2025 that VMock "is being sunsetted" there and pointed students to Big Interview's resume tool. USC says VMock will be discontinued at USC on June 30, 2026. Neither source says VMock is shutting down as a company. — [Bentley CareerEdge](https://careeredge.bentley.edu/blog/2025/10/08/breaking-news-vmock-is-being-sunsetted-action-required/); [USC Career Center](https://careers.usc.edu/resources/smart-resume/)
- Purdue Global has used VMock since 2020 and served more than 15,000 students with it. — [Purdue Global newsroom](https://www.purdueglobal.edu/newsroom/ai-powered-tools-expand-opportunities-for-students-graduates-center-for-career-advancement/)

**Big Interview (interview practice plus resume)**
- Big Interview holds a GSA federal schedule contract (47QTCA26D004M). It covers monthly per-user plans, annual tiers for up to 50, 100 and 200 users, and a "Basic Edition" with a 200-seat minimum. — [GSA T&C PDF](https://www.gsaadvantage.gov/ref_text/47QTCA26D004M/47QTCA26D004M_BigInterview_GSA_TC.pdf)
- A third-party blog reports annual plans of **$975/yr for up to 50 learners, $1,500/yr for 100, and $2,200/yr for 200 with SSO**. This is unverified on Big Interview's site, though the tier structure matches the GSA filing. — [US Tech Automations blog](https://ustechautomations.com/resources/blog/best-career-services-software-for-small-colleges-2027)
- Big Interview's enterprise page offers "custom pricing" and claims 700+ customers, including universities. — [Big Interview enterprise pricing](https://biginterview.com/pricing/enterprise)
- Consumer prices are about $39 for 30 days, $99 for 3 months, or $299 lifetime. Sources conflict, and another lists $79/mo. — [Final Round AI](https://www.finalroundai.com/blog/big-interview-pricing); [Copilot Interview](https://copilotinterview.com/blog/big-interview-pricing)
- Loyola Chicago and Mississippi State provide it to students at no cost through an institutional license. — [Loyola Chicago](https://services.luc.edu/TDClient/33/Portal/Requests/Service/283/BigInterview); [MSU](https://www.career.msstate.edu/biginterview/)

**Quinncia**
- Florida Gulf Coast University offers Quinncia through a **course textbook fee of $16 per student**. This model passes the cost to students and bypasses the career center budget. — [FGCU Career Services](https://careerservices.fgcu.edu/resources/quinncia/)

**Interstride (international students)**
- UW Tech Fee: **$15,000** request for Interstride, awarded in full. — [UW Tech Fee](https://techfee.uw.edu/?p=3351) (as summarized in search results)

**Yoodli (AI roleplay and interview coach)**
- UW gives all students, faculty and staff free Yoodli access with a UW email at yoodli.ai/uw, paid from the Student Technology Fee. It is listed in UW-IT's software catalog for UW Seattle and Bothell. The contract amount was not found. — [UW Career Center blog](https://careers.uw.edu/blog/2025/06/26/yoodli-workplace-ready-communication-skills/); [UW-IT UWare](https://it.uw.edu/uware/yoodli-ai-interview-coach/)

**Rezi (AI resume builder): the only published per-seat institutional price found**
- One Rezi page: $250/month per 1,000 users, "about $3 per student per year." Another Rezi page: $99/month per 200 job-seeker sign-ups, plus $99 for each further 200. The two rates conflict. — [Rezi career centers](https://rezi.ai/enterprise/use-cases/career-centers); [Rezi enterprise](https://www.rezi.ai/enterprise)

**Hiration, Kickresume, CareerShift, Vault/Firsthand, Teal**
- Hiration: quote-based, with "modular pricing based on selected products and student population," no usage caps, and 100+ institutions (NACE Buyers Guide). — [Hiration vs Symplicity](https://www.hiration.com/blog/symplicity-alternative-hiration-higher-ed-career-centers/); [NACE Buyers Guide](https://naceweb.org/buyers-guide/details?ID=107484)
- CareerShift: the cost to a school "depends on the type of organization, the size... and the population of users." — [CareerShift FAQ](https://www.careershift.com/About/FAQ)
- Vault (formerly Firsthand): campus-wide licenses at Columbia, Rutgers, Princeton and UCLA. Consumer Gold is $59.95 per year. Institutional price not found. — [UCLA Vault](https://ucla.firsthand.co/subscriptions/gold); [Rutgers](https://www.libraries.rutgers.edu/databases/online_career_library)
- Teal: offers volume pricing and custom contracts for 50+ users, per a secondary source. No school pricing found. — [frontdeskreview](https://frontdeskreview.com/software/resume-builders/teal.md)
- Prentus (an AI career coaching platform) sells a "flat institutional fee, no per-seat pricing." This is a self-promotional vendor blog. — [Prentus](https://prentus.com/blog/best-ai-career-coaching-software-for-colleges-and-universities)

**Career center budgets and AI adoption**
- NACE 2024-25 benchmarks: median career center budget about **$504,000** (up 21% in 2 years). Median personnel budget $401,000, about 87% of the total. — [NACE](https://naceweb.org/career-development/trends-and-predictions/career-center-budgets-climb-21-percent-in-last-two-years)
- NACE Quick Poll (Aug–Sep 2025, n=448): **76% of career centers use AI** with individual students, up from about 20% in spring 2023. Of those not using it, 64% cite lack of staff expertise and **27% cite concerns about AI collecting student personal data**. — [NACE AI poll](https://naceweb.org/career-development/trends-and-predictions/nace-quick-poll-use-of-ai-by-career-centers-to-help-students-is-growing)

### Inferences
- Non-personnel spend at a median career center is about $100k per year: $504k minus $401k, my own calculation. That money covers the CSM platform (about $8–10k for Handshake), events, travel and every bolt-on tool. A new tool at a median school realistically competes for **$2k–$15k**, and $25k+ deals happen at large schools, often through student tech fee money rather than the center's own budget.
- Small liberal arts colleges (1–3k students) are likely at the bottom of the range. Big Interview's 50/100/200-learner tiers ($975–$2,200) and Rezi's roughly $3 per student per year (about $3–9k for a 1–3k-student school) bracket a realistic price for them.
- Churn among incumbents (VMock dropped at Bentley and USC) shows schools are actively swapping AI tools. That creates openings, and it also means renewals are not safe.
- None of the tools found is an application tracker or autofill extension. Career centers buy resume review, interview practice and content. Unnepo's features (tracking, autofill, outreach) would be a new category for most buyers, so the pitch would need a justification, such as outcomes data or "staff reach" as in John Jay's VMock case.

### Gaps
- No public institutional prices found for Handshake Premium, Quinncia, CareerShift, Vault, Yoodli, Hiration, Kickresume for Schools, Teal or Careerflow for schools, Symplicity, uConnect or Purple Briefcase.
- No size-segmented breakdown (small vs large schools) of NACE career center budgets or tech spend; that is in the paid NACE report ($285 for members).
- "Handshake Premium" as a distinct university tier is not priced in any source found.

## 2. Budget cycles, procurement, who decides, sales cycle and pilots

### Takeaway
The career center director typically owns the decision, but money often comes from somewhere else, such as student technology fee committees. Tech fee proposals run on annual calls with multi-month approval timelines. Any tool touching student data will face a HECVAT security questionnaire and FERPA contract terms.

### Cited Findings
- John Jay's VMock renewal went through the student tech fee process: submitted **April 28, 2026**, approved **August 11, 2026**, about 3.5 months for a *renewal*. — [John Jay Tech Fee](https://doitapps2.jjay.cuny.edu/techfee/project_detail.php?project_id=418)
- UW's Student Technology Fee runs annual calls for proposals (a "Fall 2026" call is referenced). VMock, Interstride and Yoodli were all funded this way, not from the career center's base budget. — [UW Tech Fee](https://techfee.uw.edu); [UW Career Center blog on Yoodli](https://careers.uw.edu/blog/2025/06/26/yoodli-workplace-ready-communication-skills/)
- FGCU funds Quinncia through a $16 course textbook fee, an alternative path that avoids the career center budget. — [FGCU](https://careerservices.fgcu.edu/resources/quinncia/)
- HECVAT, the EDUCAUSE vendor security questionnaire, is "the higher education sector's preferred evaluation framework." Schools increasingly expect HECVAT 4. Montclair State requires all vendors to be assessed, and in Excel format for version 3.x+. — [EDUCAUSE HECVAT](https://www.educause.edu/higher-education-community-vendor-assessment-toolkit); [Montclair](https://inside.montclair.edu/administration/information-technology-division/information-security/vendor-assessment-hecvat); [UpGuard](https://www.upguard.com/blog/free-template-hecvat)
- Institutions negotiate FERPA "school official" contract terms, no-training-on-data clauses and exit/data-return rights with AI vendors. The source is a vendor's course, so treat it as indicative. — [ibl.ai](https://ibl.ai/courses/higher-education/ai-vendor-evaluation-procurement-higher-ed)
- EDUCAUSE published a 2026 guide on aligning vendor and higher-ed procurement strategies. — [EDUCAUSE 2026](https://www.educause.edu/content/2026/aligning-procurement-strategies-a-guide-for-vendors-and-higher-education-professionals)
- Generic edtech founder advice: founders should close the first 15–20 customers personally. Start with one campus wedge and demo to the person who can approve the purchase. Treat attention without payment as no traction. — [Insightly / Lou Shipley](https://www.insightly.com/episodes/founder-led-sales/); [Nebula Startup School](https://www.nebulastartupschool.in/blog/student-founders-campus-sales-team)

### Inferences
- Realistic timeline for a first paid contract: one semester to a full academic year. Even a routine renewal took about 3.5 months at John Jay. A new vendor needs a demo, a pilot, a security review (HECVAT) and a budget line or tech fee proposal on top of that.
- Small deals under purchasing-card or small-purchase thresholds (often a few thousand dollars) likely avoid formal RFPs. Pricing a starter tier under about $5k is a common tactic to get a director to sign directly. I found no source giving specific thresholds, so this is an inference.
- An autofill extension that reads job pages and stores student application data will draw extra scrutiny in a HECVAT review compared with a standalone web app.

### Gaps
- No source on typical university fiscal years (often July 1 to June 30) or on purchasing-card or RFP thresholds; these vary by school and need to be checked school by school.
- No survey data on average career-tech sales cycle length or pilot-to-paid conversion rates.
- No primary source (LinkedIn posts or interviews with directors) on how directors evaluate new vendors.

## 3. How AI-era startups broke into career centers and how long it took

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin. The visible pattern is flagship-school anchor deals funded by tech fees (Yoodli and VMock at UW), consumer products that add an institutional tier (Big Interview, Rezi, Teal), and replacing an incumbent being dropped (Big Interview replacing VMock at Bentley).

### Cited Findings
- Yoodli: UW's career center has promoted it since at least January 2025 (interview roleplay) and kept promoting it through 2025–2026, funded by the student tech fee. Yoodli's other visible partnerships are corporate, such as @ASCEND coaching in March 2025. — [UW Jan 2025](https://careers.uw.edu/blog/2025/01/08/test-tags/); [UW Jun 2025](https://careers.uw.edu/blog/2025/06/26/yoodli-workplace-ready-communication-skills/); [Yoodli × Ascend](https://yoodli.ai/blog/yoodli-and-ascend-partner-to-revolutionize-interview-executive-presence-coaching)
- Big Interview won Bentley's resume-review slot when VMock was dropped there in October 2025. — [Bentley](https://careeredge.bentley.edu/blog/2025/10/08/breaking-news-vmock-is-being-sunsetted-action-required/)
- Hiration claims 100+ institutions and markets head-to-head pages against Symplicity and VMock. — [Hiration vs VMock](https://www.hiration.com/blog/vmock-alternative-hiration-for-career-centers/); [NACE Buyers Guide](https://naceweb.org/buyers-guide/details?ID=107484)
- Rezi sells a self-serve, white-label enterprise tier with published per-seat pricing, a product-led route to schools. — [Rezi enterprise](https://www.rezi.ai/enterprise)
- Newer entrants (Qwyse, Prentus, CareerVillage COACH, Web Scribble) are actively marketing to career centers in 2026, which suggests a crowded AI-tool category. — [Qwyse comparison](https://www.qwyse.com/blog/ai-career-platform-universities-qwyse-vmock-quinncia-big-interview/); [Prentus](https://prentus.com/blog/best-ai-career-coaching-software-for-colleges-and-universities); [HigherEdDive press release May 2026](https://www.highereddive.com/press-release/20260512-web-scribble-launches-career-center-technology-for-colleges-and-universitie/)
- NACE's Buyers Guide is the category's directory of vendors, which suggests a listing there is a standard channel. — [NACE Buyers Guide](https://naceweb.org/buyers-guide/details?ID=107484)

### Inferences
- The fastest visible path is one school where the founder has access (their own college), a free pilot for a semester, then a paid renewal. Usage data is then the case study, and the founder replicates the deal at peer schools. Expect about 6–12 months before institutional revenue is meaningful.
- Many AI vendors are now pitching the same directors, so a generic "AI resume or cover letter tool" will struggle to stand out. Application tracking plus outcome data could be a hook career centers lack, and directors need outcomes data for reporting. Whether directors will pay for it is untested.

### Gaps
- No founder interviews found with explicit time-to-first-contract for Yoodli, Hiration or Kickresume in higher ed.
- No data on how many schools Yoodli, Rezi or Teal have signed, or on their institutional revenue.

## 4. Other paying institutions: bootcamps, MBA programs, clubs, Greek life, athletics, international offices, workforce programs

### Takeaway
There are documented examples for business schools, finance clubs and international student offices. Bootcamps, Greek life, athletics and workforce programs had no pricing evidence. Club deals are small, a few hundred to a few thousand dollars.

### Cited Findings
- Wall Street Prep sells club and school "passports." A Michigan State finance association deal priced the catalog at $300 per student instead of $999. LMU's business school gets access for students at a "deeply reduced fee" of $50. — [WSP MSU](https://wallstreetprep.com/passport/msu-2023); [WSP FMA 2024-25](https://wallstreetprep.com/passport/financial-management-association-2024-25); [LMU CBA](https://cba.lmu.edu/academics/undergrad/finance/studentsuccess/wallstreetprep/)
- WallStreetMojo runs an institutional partnership program for departments, batches, clubs, bootcamps and placement programs. Pricing is not listed. — [WallStreetMojo](https://www.wallstreetmojo.com/?p=53088)
- Columbia SIPA's career center maintains a list of paid subscription databases bought for one professional school, which suggests individual schools within a university buy separately. — [Columbia SIPA](https://sipa.columbia.edu/career-advancement-center/career-resources/online-databases-paid-subscriptions)
- An international student office buyer exists: UW funded Interstride at $15k. — [UW Tech Fee](https://techfee.uw.edu/?p=3351)
- Workforce and government: Big Interview's GSA schedule shows a federal channel with seat tiers. — [GSA](https://www.gsaadvantage.gov/ref_text/47QTCA26D004M/47QTCA26D004M_BigInterview_GSA_TC.pdf)

### Inferences
- Finance and consulting clubs, departmental programs (business schools, engineering co-op offices) and international offices are smaller, faster buyers. Typical deals are likely about $500–$5k, decided by one person with discretionary or club funds. They are useful as wedges but cannot reach $120k ARR alone.
- Charging students a discount through the club (the WSP passport model) is effectively a consumer acquisition channel more than a B2B channel.

### Gaps
- No pricing or buying evidence found for bootcamps, Greek life, athletics departments or WIOA/workforce boards buying job-application tools.
- No data on student club budgets.

## 5. Math: contracts needed for $10k MRR ($120k ARR)

### Takeaway
At realistic price points for a new vendor, $120k ARR takes about 12–50 institutional contracts, or about 5 at VMock-level prices. Given sales cycles of a semester or more, institutional revenue is unlikely to reach $120k within 12 months for a solo founder without an existing network.

### Cited Findings (price anchors)
- About $1k–2.2k/yr per 50–200 learners (Big Interview tiers, unverified). — [US Tech Automations](https://ustechautomations.com/resources/blog/best-career-services-software-for-small-colleges-2027)
- About $3 per student per year (Rezi). — [Rezi](https://rezi.ai/enterprise/use-cases/career-centers)
- About $8–10k/yr (Handshake, university side). — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/2f92db9f-5d2a-4814-a0df-aef6277059b0/)
- $15k (Interstride, UW), $25k (VMock, John Jay), $115.8k (VMock, UW 3 campuses). — [UW](https://techfee.uw.edu/?p=3351); [John Jay](https://doitapps2.jjay.cuny.edu/techfee/project_detail.php?project_id=418); [UW VMock](https://techfee.uw.edu/proposal/vmock-smart-career-platform-uniform-access-block/)

### Inferences (my arithmetic)
| Annual contract price | Contracts for $120k ARR | Typical buyer |
|---|---|---|
| $1,000 | 120 | Club, small department |
| $2,500 | 48 | Small college pilot tier, MBA club |
| $5,000 | 24 | Small/mid college career center |
| $10,000 | 12 | Mid-size college (Handshake-level spend) |
| $25,000 | ~5 | Large public, VMock-level (John Jay) |

- Per-seat comparison: at $3 per student per year (Rezi), a 2,000-student liberal arts college is only about $6k, so reaching $120k would need about 40,000 licensed students.
- Consumer comparison: at about $10–15/mo, $10k MRR needs roughly 670–1,000 paying students. One $5k school contract equals about 30–40 consumer subscribers for a year.
- A blended path is plausible: consumer revenue now, plus 3–5 pilot schools in 2027 that convert to $5–10k each, which would be about $25–50k ARR from B2B in year one.

### Gaps
- No data on win rates or pilot conversion rates for career-tech vendors.

## 6. Risks: slow cycles, data privacy (FERPA), seat utilization, churn

### Takeaway
The main risks are long, calendar-bound cycles, security and privacy reviews (HECVAT/FERPA, AI data-use concerns), low student activation (which drives non-renewal), and active vendor churn and consolidation.

### Cited Findings
- 27% of career centers not using AI cite concerns about AI collecting student data, and 64% cite lack of staff expertise. — [NACE poll 2025](https://naceweb.org/career-development/trends-and-predictions/nace-quick-poll-use-of-ai-by-career-centers-to-help-students-is-growing)
- John Jay's VMock renewal said it "will use data from VMOCK" in future but reported no current usage figures. Renewals are justified on reach, not proven usage. — [John Jay](https://doitapps2.jjay.cuny.edu/techfee/project_detail.php?project_id=418)
- Incumbent churn: VMock was dropped at Bentley (Oct 2025) and USC (June 2026). — [Bentley](https://careeredge.bentley.edu/blog/2025/10/08/breaking-news-vmock-is-being-sunsetted-action-required/); [USC](https://careers.usc.edu/resources/smart-resume/)
- Buyers are advised to request verified adoption rates from comparable institutions before signing and to check SSO and data handling at termination. This comes from a vendor blog. — [Prentus](https://prentus.com/blog/best-ai-career-coaching-software-for-colleges-and-universities)
- HECVAT review is required by schools such as Montclair for all vendors. — [Montclair](https://inside.montclair.edu/administration/information-technology-division/information-security/vendor-assessment-hecvat)
- Fewer than one-third of Class of 2025 seniors used AI in their job search (NACE). That is low organic adoption, so career centers would need to drive usage. — [NACE poll](https://naceweb.org/career-development/trends-and-predictions/nace-quick-poll-use-of-ai-by-career-centers-to-help-students-is-growing)

### Inferences
- Seat utilization is the renewal risk. Tools are typically "free to students," so the center needs usage reports to justify renewal. Unnepo's tracking data (applications sent, follow-ups) could serve as that usage report.
- Bundling risk: Handshake and Symplicity are the CSM platforms every school already pays for. Any feature they add (AI resume or tracking) becomes free to the school and threatens bolt-on vendors. I found no direct source on their 2026 AI roadmaps.
- A Chrome extension that reads third-party ATS pages may raise IT-policy questions: managed Chromebooks, extension allowlists, and data flow to an AI provider. Expect to need a HECVAT, a privacy policy with no-training-on-student-data terms, and SSO for anything beyond a pilot.

### Gaps
- No public seat-utilization or activation benchmarks for career tools (for example, % of students activating VMock or Big Interview).
- No sources on Handshake's or Symplicity's 2026 AI feature roadmaps or pricing changes.
